# 1:1 Ticketing Experience Pilot

<!-- meta -->
**Date:** November 2024 – Jan 2025

**Role:** Research Lead

**Team:** Design technologists · Product managers · Engineers

**Client:** IMAX 

<!-- intro -->
A rapid-cycle ideation with IMAX's product team surfaced 8 concepts for direct-to-consumer services to improve engagement and conversion.

---

## Context

IMAX wanted to explore how AI could improve the ticketing experience on IMAX.com and beyond. The brief was broad: "AI Ticketing Experience." But the underlying business problem was sharper—ticket sales were Fandango's domain (IMAX received affiliate fees only), meaning any AI investment had to drive demand without owning transaction data.

We were tasked with rapid concept development and testing to determine if Q4 2024 MVP was viable. This meant learning fast, making visible progress, and managing client expectations in a space where the problem wasn't fully defined.

---

## Approach

We structured the engagement as a tight discovery cycle: internal alignment, stakeholder workshop, and post-workshop scope clarification.

- **Mixed Methods User research**: Understood the flow from movie discovery to ticketing, identifying key pain points related to IMAX movie purchasing.

- **Kick-off & data inventory**: Collaborated with client to map available data sources (18 potential feeds: CMS, showtimes, behavior, external ratings, loyalty, etc.). Clarified constraints upfront (Fandango partnership meant zero purchase data).

- **90-minute client workshop**: Facilitated interactive ideation across client stakeholder group (PM, design, transformation officer). Presented 6+ concept territories by problem area. Captured real-time feedback; refined ideas in-flight.

- **Post-workshop strategy alignment**: Internal debrief and reframing to clarify MVP fidelity, manage scope creep expectations, and align on UX philosophy (frictionless vs. delightful).

The approach was iterative by design—we moved from ideation to client feedback to internal clarification in 2 weeks. This velocity required clear ownership (who owns agenda? presentation? whiteboard?), strong async documentation, and willingness to adapt on the fly.

---

## Key findings

- **Frictionless wins over delightful**: Client's post-workshop email suggested visual helpers and character-driven UX. But user research from prior project indicated people wanted *fast ticket purchase*, not engaging chatbot. Repositioned strategy: "IMAX.com enables the most frictionless ticket-buying experience by getting fans a link to purchase tickets for the right movie at exactly the right time."

- **Eight viable concept territories emerged**: Location-based theater finder; movie-format alerts; personality-driven recommendations; loyalty integration; social group ticketing; Letterboxd sync; genre-based discovery; early-access ticketing. Client enthusiasm across territories created scope clarity challenge.

- **Client approval ≠ alignment**: Week-1 concept approval by Lauren was energizing but masked misalignment on MVP fidelity. She was already discussing next phases while core questions remained: Is this interactive prototype or production-ready? Who's building? What's success?

- **Stakeholder heterogeneity matters**: Chief Transformation Officer brought energy and strategic insight ("IMAX is scarce like concert tickets—scarcity is leverage"). Finance/product stakeholders came with constraints (international scope, data access, technical debt). Learning to speak both languages prevented gridlock.

---

## Impact

<!-- metrics -->
- 8 | Product concept territories validated and documented
- 1 | 90-min client workshop with 8+ stakeholder participants
- 6 | Internal design/research/tech alignment sessions
- 1 | Strategic repositioning (frictionless vs. delightful UX)
- 1 | Shift Lab tech partnership established; Contentful CMS access enabled

The immediate impact was confidence-building for the client. A vague brief ("do something with AI ticketing") transformed into 8 concrete explorations, each with data sources, feasibility notes, and rough value propositions. The workshop itself became a forcing function for internal client alignment—they discovered gaps between what PM wanted vs. what design imagined vs. what finance could fund.

The longer-term impact was strategic. By reframing the solution from "delightful AI companion" to "frictionless ticket discovery," we shifted design conversations toward the actual user need (speed, confidence) rather than the feature fantasy (character interaction). This set the tone for MVP scope and execution phase.

---

## Reflections

The project highlighted a recurring pattern: approval is not synonymous with alignment. Enthusiasm post-workshop was genuine, but it masked unresolved questions about MVP fidelity, resource allocation, and success criteria. In hindsight, scheduling a structured debrief meeting *before* the workshop (to align on what approval would mean) would have prevented the Oct 4 "shell shock" moment.

The frictionless-vs.-delightful tension was real and worth surfacing. The temptation was to agree with everything Lauren suggested and iterate toward her vision. Instead, we grounded the UX philosophy in prior user research and user needs. This required confidence and trust-building—we weren't saying "no," we were saying "here's why user research says this matters more."

### What I would have done differently
Schedule a pre-workshop stakeholder alignment call to define what "approval" means (prototype fidelity? concept clarity? resource commitment?). Front-load the UX philosophy conversation rather than surfacing it post-workshop. Create a more explicit handoff between discovery and execution phase—the Jan 2025 messages suggest confusion about scope still lingering.
